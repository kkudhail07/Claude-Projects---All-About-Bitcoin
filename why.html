The case for Bitcoin

Why Bitcoin?

Knowing what Bitcoin is matters less than knowing why it might matter to you. Here's the "so what" — in plain terms, no finance degree required.

A money no one can print

Cash slowly loses value as more of it is created — your savings buy a little less each year. Bitcoin's supply is capped at 21 million and can't be inflated, so it's designed to hold value over the long run.

Send value anywhere

Move money to anyone, anywhere, at any hour — no bank, no border, no waiting days. For sending money home across the world, it can be far cheaper and faster than the old way.

Open to everyone

Around the world, billions of people can't get a bank account. All you need for Bitcoin is a phone and an internet connection — no paperwork, no permission, no one to turn you away.

Truly your own

Held properly, your bitcoin is yours alone — no account that can be frozen, no company that can fail with your money inside. You don't have to trust a middleman to look after it.

None of this makes Bitcoin a sure thing — it's young, and its price swings hard. But these are the reasons thoughtful people keep paying attention.

An important distinction

Bitcoin isn't "crypto"

You'll often see Bitcoin lumped in with thousands of other "cryptocurrencies" — coins with founders, marketing teams, and prices that pump and crash. It's worth separating Bitcoin from that crowd, because it's a genuinely different thing.

Bitcoin

  • No company, founder or CEO in charge — its creator left and is anonymous.
  • A fixed, knowable supply of 21 million.
  • Launched fairly in 2009 with no pre-mine or insider allocation.
  • Run by tens of thousands of independent computers, not a head office.

Most other "crypto"

  • Usually has a company and team that can change the rules.
  • Supply is often flexible — more tokens can be created.
  • Frequently launched with a big share kept by insiders.
  • Ranges from serious projects to outright memecoins and scams.

A simple rule of thumb for newcomers: learn Bitcoin first. Understand it on its own before wading into the wider, noisier world around it.
